# Western honey bee colony notes

Apis mellifera colonies overwinter as a cluster around the queen. Workers generate heat by shivering flight muscles. A healthy cluster in a Langstroth hive should cover at least six frames in January in temperate climates.

## Queen pheromone

The queen mandibular pheromone (QMP) suppresses worker ovary development. If QMP drops because the queen is failing, workers start emergency queen cells on young larvae, usually on day 3 after the drop is detectable.

## Forage and stores

A colony needs roughly 20 kg of honey to overwinter in a cold climate. Spring nectar dearths in April can starve a rapidly expanding brood nest even when frames look full of bees. Beekeepers should leave two frames of capped honey next to the brood.

## Varroa

Varroa destructor mites reproduce in capped brood. A sugar-roll sample over 3% mites per 100 bees in August requires treatment before winter bees are reared. Oxalic acid vapor is used when little brood is present; formic acid pads can be used with brood if temperatures stay between 10°C and 29°C.

## Swarming

Colonies swarm after they raise queen cells and the old queen leaves with roughly half the workers. Swarm prep signs include drone brood, backfilling of nectar in the brood nest, and cups along the bottom of frames. Splitting the hive before cells are capped is the usual prevention.
